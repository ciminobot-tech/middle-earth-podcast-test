# Episode 1 — Before Sauron

**Narrator:** Isabella (`bf_isabella`)  
**Core running time:** about 9 minutes at a calm 145 words a minute  
**Optional listener paths:** about 2 minutes each  
**Purpose:** explain what existed before Sauron, who Morgoth was, and why the One Ring matters — without assuming any lore knowledge.

## Opening — 0:00–1:15

Welcome. Before there was a Ring, before there was a hobbit carrying it through Mordor, there was a world already scarred by a much older enemy.

This is Middle-earth in one important sense: it is not a story that begins with a map, a king, or a war. It begins with a world being made, and then changed by a being who wanted to possess it.

For this first journey, you only need three names. Eru Ilúvatar is Tolkien's creator. The Valar are powerful beings who enter the world to shape and care for it. And Morgoth is the first great rebel among them. Long before Sauron becomes the Dark Lord we know, he serves Morgoth.

If you know the films, think of Sauron as the later and smaller heir to an older disaster. If you know none of this, that is perfect. We will meet each person before we need them.

**Listener stop:** Want the simple version of Morgoth, the deeper creation story, or to carry on?

## The first shadow — 1:15–3:20

Morgoth's earlier name is Melkor. He is one of the greatest of the Ainur — spirits made before the physical world. His problem is not simply that he likes destruction. He wants to make things answer to him. He wants his own designs to become real, even when they harm what others have made.

When the world, called Arda, is shaped, Melkor enters it too. The Valar try to make a place of order and beauty. Melkor repeatedly breaks, twists, or claims what they build. Tolkien's history is full of beautiful places that survive for a while under pressure: kingdoms, forests, hidden cities, families, and friendships.

This is why the later wars feel old. Sauron does not invent the idea of ruling Middle-earth through fear. He inherits it.

One careful note. Tolkien developed parts of this early history in more than one form. The broad picture is stable: Melkor rebels, enters the world, and becomes its first great enemy. Exact dates and some details change across later writings. We will flag those moments rather than pretend every version is one settled fact.

## The light, the jewels, and the exile — 3:20–6:20

At this stage, the main light in the western land of Aman comes from two magical trees: one silver, one gold. The Elves have awakened far away in Middle-earth. Some begin a long journey west. Among them are the Noldor, an Elvish people known for skill, learning, and craft.

Melkor is captured for a time, but he is later released under watch. He lies, creates distrust among the Noldor, and then escapes with Ungoliant, a being of hunger and darkness. Together they destroy the Two Trees. Melkor murders Finwë, one of the Noldor's kings, and steals the Silmarils.

The Silmarils are three jewels made by Fëanor, Finwë's son. They hold the light of the Trees. They are beautiful, sacred to their maker, and soon become the centre of a terrible oath. Fëanor and his sons swear to recover them from anyone who keeps them.

That oath drives the Noldor into exile and begins the long wars against Morgoth in the north-west of Middle-earth. This is the First Age: the age of Beren and Lúthien, Túrin, Gondolin, and eventually Elrond's parents. It ends only when Morgoth is defeated in a war so huge that much of the land called Beleriand is drowned beneath the sea.

That is the first big lesson: the world of *The Lord of the Rings* is built on ruins. Its old songs are not decoration. They are memories of real losses.

**Listener stop:** Why do the Silmarils matter? Who are the Noldor? Or continue to Sauron?

## Sauron steps forward — 6:20–8:35

Sauron begins as a servant of Morgoth. He is also a Maia: a lesser spirit than a Vala, but still far beyond an ordinary person. Where Morgoth is a vast tyrant and destroyer, Sauron is more of an organiser. He loves order, control, and systems that make other people obey.

After Morgoth's defeat, Sauron is not destroyed. For a time, he hides. Then he returns in the Second Age and tries a different method. Rather than conquer everyone at once, he presents himself in a fair and helpful form. He teaches the Elven smiths of Eregion new craft.

Together, the smiths make Rings of Power. But Sauron has a secret plan. In Mordor he makes one Ring in secret: the One Ring. Its purpose is to rule the other Rings and to bring their wearers under his power.

The Elves understand the danger quickly. They remove their Three Rings before Sauron can use the One to master them. But the damage is already done. Other Rings go to Dwarf-lords and to Men. Nine Men who receive Rings become the Nazgûl, Sauron's most feared servants.

## Why the One Ring matters — 8:35–11:20

The One Ring is not just a very dangerous magic object. It contains a large part of Sauron's own power. That gives it two effects at once.

First, while Sauron has it, it helps him dominate the other Ring-bearers. Second, if he loses it, he is weakened, but not erased. He can rebuild. This is why the Ring is both a weapon and a trap. It promises its bearer power, but it pulls them toward Sauron's way of thinking: possess, command, and use people as tools.

When Isildur cuts the Ring from Sauron's hand at the end of the Second Age, he has a chance to destroy it. He does not. The Ring is lost for centuries, found by Déagol, taken by Sméagol, and finally passed to Bilbo. By the time Frodo inherits it, the old war is returning.

This is why Gandalf is so afraid of something that can fit on a chain. The Ring is a surviving piece of an ancient ruler. If Sauron gets it back, he does not merely gain a powerful object. He becomes whole in the way that matters most for his rule.

**Listener stop:** Why could Frodo carry it? What happened to Isildur? Or go deeper on the other Rings?

## The world before the quest — 11:20–13:30

By the beginning of *The Lord of the Rings*, Middle-earth is not a single country. Gondor is a great but tired kingdom in the south. Rohan is its horse-lord ally. The Shire is a small, sheltered Hobbit land in the north-west. Rivendell and Lórien are Elvish refuges, holding on to beauty that is slowly fading. Dwarves have returned to the Lonely Mountain. Sauron is gathering strength in Mordor, with allies and armies across a much larger world.

Frodo's journey matters because none of these places can simply use the Ring safely. The wise fear it. The powerful would be changed by it. And the weak can be hurt by it. The task is not to use the ultimate weapon better than Sauron. It is to refuse the logic behind it.

Next, we can choose our road. We can follow the Ring from Isildur to Frodo. We can travel back into the First Age and meet the people behind the old songs. Or we can map the powers at war when the story begins.

For now, keep one thought: Sauron is frightening because he is ancient and powerful. But the story began earlier, with Morgoth, and with the first wish to own the world rather than share it.

## Optional answer cards

### What is a Maia? — about 2 minutes

A Maia is one of the lesser Ainur: still a spiritual being of great power, but beneath the Valar. Gandalf, Saruman, Sauron, and Melian are all Maiar. “Lesser” is a rank, not a claim that they are unimportant. A Maia can change the course of an age.

### Did the Elves make the One Ring? — about 2 minutes

No. The Elven smiths of Eregion made many Rings of Power, including the Three. Sauron secretly made the One Ring alone in Mordor. The Three were not touched by him directly, but because they were made with knowledge gained through his teaching, the One could still threaten their keepers.

### Is Morgoth the same as Sauron? — about 2 minutes

No. Morgoth is the older master and first great enemy. Sauron serves him in the First Age and later continues his project on a smaller scale. They are linked, but they are not the same being.
